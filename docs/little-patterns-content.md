# Little Patterns: all game content and answers

This reference is generated from learning.js and core.js. It contains all built-in answers and content for Count, Add, Words, Patterns, Shapes and Colour Blocks, with no player data. To change sentences, word pools, picture symbols or shapes, edit learning.js; sums, patterns and shapes are generated by sumRound, patternRound and shapeRound. Run node tools/build-little-patterns-content.cjs after changing the content.

## Sentences and missing-word answers

Two banks: the picnic (fourteen sentences) and bath and bed time (twelve, several of five words for Word order), all using the same symbol words. The same sentences are also used for Word order. Grown-ups can add up to twelve familiar sentences locally (missing word in capitals); those private additions cannot be listed here. Tapping the sentence says it with “blank” until the answer is checked. Each missing-word round offers its answer plus two alternatives from the listed pool; the answer is typed into the gap. The sentence picture is always visible, and the three word cards show their pictures from the start (Grown-ups › Settings › Word pictures can hide them until Help). Help! (the button with the hand sign for help) first outlines the sentence’s own picture (or shows the hidden pictures), then outlines the card that fits. The A to Z keys are in ABC order and end in Delete and Enter; the device keyboard is a Grown-ups setting. Tapping a word says it, only while sound is on.

## Every Garden addition

Add has six levels chosen from the level pill: 1 within 5 (the default), 2 within 10, 3 within 20 without crossing ten (10 + 4), 4 within 20 crossing ten (8 + 5), 5 tens to 100 (30 + 20), 6 the missing part (2 + ? = 5, addition only, within ten, with the joined group shown and the hidden part outlined). Level 1 is the part of level 2 with totals up to 5, so it is not listed separately. Every sum is one picture: the first group, a plus sign, the second group inside a dashed outline, the equation under it with a ? for the answer, and three answers (levels 1 to 4 offer the answer and two near neighbours, smallest first). Groups over five sit in rows of five. Help first counts every apple across both groups in one count (the new apples drop in as they are counted), then outlines the answer. Levels 3 and 4 draw two ten-frames, the added one dashed; at level 4 Help fills the ten first (“Fill the ten first.”) and counts on. Level 5 draws complete tens as rows of ten apples. Doubles and Make ten are sets inside Add. The picker allows every sum in the level; reversed groups are separate examples. Play order within a level is deliberately mixed so the next answer cannot be predicted from the last; this table is sorted for reading.

## Every Garden take away

Take away lives inside the Add activity behind an Add / Take away switch and shares its levels: 1 from up to 5, 2 from up to 10, 3 from 11 to 19 taking ones only (15 − 3), 4 within 20 crossing ten (12 − 5), 5 tens (60 − 20). Nook starts with one group and eats none, some or all of it: the eaten apples turn pale and are crossed out from the end of the same group, and after the answer they vanish while the rest stay where they were (Nook munches on a right answer). Levels 1 to 4 offer the answer and two near neighbours. Zero is included both as an amount taken and as an answer. Level 1 is the part of level 2 starting from up to 5.

## Every Shapes example

Shapes has two chips, Sides and Name, and one level pill shared by both. Level 1: triangles, squares and rectangles (and in Name, circles and ovals), gently turned; level 2 adds pentagons and hexagons, including uneven and rotated ones. Sides shows polygons only: the child taps each side (a tap within about 22 CSS pixels of a side counts it once and numbers it in tap order; a counted side is never counted twice), and three number answers are there from the start. Name shows three words; Help first puts a small picture beside each word, then outlines the answer. SQUARE and RECTANGLE are never offered together, nor CIRCLE and OVAL at level 1. A right answer says “We did it!” and the fact: “A pentagon has five sides.” or “It’s a hexagon!”. 33 examples; the answers offered are fixed per example.
